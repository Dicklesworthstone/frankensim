# Worked example: a finned heatsink with a declared fan

This is the deepest walkthrough the product supports today, and the first
one that runs the whole pipeline on a real body. It declares the full cooling
contract — fan system, vent area, airflow-leakage bypass — and the solid side
of the problem: a seeded aluminium region and an `airflow-convection`
boundary whose heat-transfer coefficient is DERIVED at solve time from the
fan network's operating point through a named `fs-convection` card. All seven
solve stages execute; `report` and `package` export exactly the bytes the
run retained.

```bash
cargo run -p fs-cli --bin frankensim -- --json validate examples/heatsink-fan/heatsink-fan.fsim
```

Expected: exit 0, `"status":"ok"`, `"finding_count":0`. The G0/G1 battery
(`crates/fs-cli/tests/cli.rs`,
`g1_the_heatsink_fan_example_runs_every_stage_through_the_real_cli_verb`)
executes validate, import, and `run` continuously against exactly these
bytes, so this walkthrough cannot silently rot.

## The geometry

`heatsink.stl` (ASCII, 108 facets) is ONE closed manifold shell in metres,
generated by the tracked `generate_heatsink_stl.py`:

| Part | Extents (x, y, z) |
|------|-------------------|
| Base plate | 80 × 60 × 5 mm at the origin |
| Fin k = 0..3 | 6 × 60 × 20 mm, x-offsets 8 + 18k mm, standing on the base |

The comb profile lives in the x–z plane and is extruded along y. Every
facet is an axis-aligned rectangle of the breakpoint grid cut on one
diagonal — no T-junctions, no slivers — and the generator asserts the shell
is closed with outward normals and the analytic volume (52.8 cm³). Import
admits it with `--max-hole-edges 0` (zero boundary edges — checkable
arithmetic, not a claim of manufacturability).

An earlier revision of this example shipped five separate boxes glued along
coincident faces. That soup imports, but it is not a volume: the conduction
stage refused it at constrained volumetricization, honestly. The single-shell
body is what the physics needs.

## The cooling declaration

- **Fan system**: one series bank whose curve is a two-point fixture line
  (0 Pa at 0.002 m³/s, 120 Pa at zero flow), run at `speed-ratio 0.7`. The
  `source` string says plainly that this is NOT manufacturer data — an
  example may teach the schema with a declared synthetic curve; a product
  study may not. The 70 % speed setting is what keeps the channel flow
  laminar (see the card's domain below); at full speed the same declaration
  is refused, not approximated.
- **Vent**: 0.00072 m² — the sum of the three inter-fin channels
  (3 × 12 mm × 20 mm). An earlier revision wrote 0.0004 m² beside the same
  parenthetical; the arithmetic is now consistent with the geometry.
- **Airflow leakage**: 0.0001 m² declared bypass (an imperfect shroud).
  Absence here would make the flow-network stage refuse rather than invent
  a path; declaring a small honest bypass is what lets the operating point
  execute.
- **Power**: 3 W at duty 1.0 dissipated in the metal region — a volumetric
  simplification of a chip heating the base, documented as such.

## The conduction declaration

- **Region seed**: `metal` is seeded at `(0.04, 0.03, 0.0025)` m — inside
  the 5 mm base plate. The volumetricizer grows the labelled tet volume from
  that seed; a seed in the air between fins would be refused, not guessed.
- **Boundary**: one `airflow-convection` row on `metal`, fed by branch
  `metal` (the declared vent) at order 0, with the channel geometry the
  correlation needs: hydraulic diameter 15 mm (a 12 × 20 mm channel with
  the shroud as its fourth wall), flow area 0.00072 m², channel length
  60 mm along the fins, and the 293.15 K inlet. The coefficient and the
  air-side temperature are derived from the flow-network operating point;
  they are not declared anywhere in this file.
- **Correlation**: `convection.circular-duct-hausen-developing` — laminar,
  thermally developing duct flow. It is the one admitted card whose
  validity domain covers a channel this short (L/Dh = 4; the developed-flow
  cards require L/Dh ≥ 10) and it requires Re ≤ 2300. The receipt of the
  reference run records the operating point inside that domain
  (Re ≈ 1.5 × 10³ at 70 % speed; at full speed Re ≈ 2.2 × 10³, too close
  to the ceiling to teach with). The numbers in this paragraph describe the
  retained receipt, not this file; read the receipt.
- **Requirement and output**: a sourced `temperature-max` limit of
  353.15 K with a 5 K margin on `metal`, and the matching scalar output.

## What solve does with this

```
import-verify → assign → material-resolve → flow-network → conduction → qoi → report
```

- **flow-network** certifies the fan/vent/leakage operating point with
  interval arithmetic — the stage whose output carries outward-rounded
  arithmetic authority end to end.
- **conduction** volumetricizes the shell (constrained Delaunay with
  segment and facet recovery, independently audited by exact winding
  numbers against the declared surface), builds the derived Robin row from
  the card, iterates the solid/air exchange to a fixed point, and closes the
  energy balance. The receipt records the branch, the card, whether the
  operating point sat inside the card's domain, the derived coefficient, the
  air temperature rise, and the balance residual.
- **qoi** extracts the region maximum and composes it with the sourced
  limit. Every one of the eight engineering-uncertainty terms is an explicit
  `NO-DATA`, so the verdict is **Estimated / indeterminate** — the product is
  honest that nothing here is a compliance decision.
- **report** projects the retained receipts into an HTML report, a JSON
  twin, and a format-9 evidence package (Estimated claims only), seals all
  three in the ledger; `report` / `package` export exactly those bytes and
  refuse an unknown run without writing anything.

Read the HTML: every number cites the receipt hash it was copied from, and
the uncertainty table prints `NO-DATA` where nothing was measured. See
`heatsink-fan-ladder.fsim` for the same project with `(solver :fidelity
"ladder")` and a 900 s time budget: the conduction stage then solves the
audited mesh and two uniform refinements (685 → 5,480 → 43,840 tets), the
receipt's `ladder` block lists every rung, and the QoI budget's discretization
term becomes a measured `interval` (on this body the maximum moves by 0.37 mK
then 0.05 mK, so the bound is the disclosed data-range one, 1.1 mK). It costs
about seventy base solves; the default `auto` fidelity solves once and says
`"stop":"fidelity-single-rung"`.

`heatsink-rotated.stl` is the same shell rotated 35° about z and 21° about x
and translated (`generate_heatsink_stl.py OUT --rotate 35 21 --shift 0.1 0.2
0.05`): every facet oblique, no coordinate dyadic, which is what real CAD
looks like. It is the rotation-invariance body of the mesh corpus
(`crates/fs-mesh/tests/body_corpus.rs`: all 108 facets recovered, exact
volume, 379 tets, smallest dihedral 1.6°; fs-mesh `CONTRACT.md` items 18–23
record the kernel, recovery, repair and import-orientation defects this one
body found) and of the product path: `heatsink-fan-rotated.fsim` is the same
project on the rotated shell, and the freshness lane solves both and requires
the two maximum temperatures to agree within 1 mK. MEASURED 2026-09-03:
axis-aligned 301.99578 K on 713 tets, rotated 301.99562 K on 704 tets, a
0.16 mK difference on an 8.85 K rise, inside the 1.1 mK discretization bound
the ladder measured. See
`examples/cooling-enclosure/README.md` for receipt anatomy and
`examples/heated-plate/README.md` for the minimal schema tour.
