Why a kitchen lab

A kitchen is heat, air and people in one small room.

At a normal rush the five kitchens here run 71 to 414 kW of cooking heat in rooms of 400 to 3,200 ft². The burners, the hood, the walk-in and the cooks share that air and the same few aisles. Drawings and spreadsheets check each part on its own; Kitchen Lab runs them together.

  • Gas or induction

    Where does the burner's heat go?

    An open gas burner puts about 42% of its heat into the pan. The rest heats the kitchen and the hood. Induction puts about 86% into the pan, so the hood and the air conditioning can be smaller.

    Compare all five kitchens

  • Walk-in cooler

    Will it stay under 41 °F?

    A rack of hot pans rolls in and the door keeps opening. Warm air pours in at the top of the doorway while cold air spills out at the floor, and frost on the coil cuts its cooling.

    Load the walk-in

  • Delivery

    Crisp, or hot enough?

    Seal a delivery box and the fries steam and go soft. Open the vent too far and the food drops below 135 °F, the hot-holding limit. Drag the vent and watch both numbers.

    Pack a delivery

  • Aisles

    Is 38 inches wide enough?

    The mall counter's tightest aisle is 38 inches. Once a tall cook bends to a low shelf, nobody else can get past. Test three body sizes in aisles from 32 to 60 inches.

    Test an aisle

01Equipment

Every piece of equipment, to the millimetre.

Ranges, combi ovens, hoods, skillets and even the steaks are built in code, part by part. Each one has its true size, doors and baskets that move, grip points for a cook's hands, and a material card that sets how it heats.

How it's made

02The stove

Sear a steak, then pour in the wine.

A ribeye sears in a skillet over gas, induction or an electric coil. Change the pan or the fat and the sear changes. Pour in 120 mL of wine and it boils on contact, cools the pan and lifts the browned bits off the bottom.

How it's computed

03The oven

Steam makes the loaf rise higher.

Steam in a hot oven condenses on the cool dough and gives up its heat. The skin stays soft for longer, so the loaf keeps rising before the crust sets. Turn the steam off and the crust locks early.

How it's computed

04Cold chain

From the walk-in to the doorstep.

In the walk-in, hot pans and an open door work against the cooling coil. In the delivery box, the vent trades heat for crispness. Both are live models, and both tell you when the food crosses a safety line.

How it's computed

05The crew

Three cooks, one aisle, a full pan.

A short, an average and a tall cook each lift a full hotel pan off a low shelf. Narrow the aisle or add weight to see who runs out of room and how hard each back works.

How it's computed

Five kitchens, side by side.

Under the hood

One model of each kitchen, from drawing to physics.

The pictures and the numbers on this page come from the same build files and solvers that FrankenSim runs in Rust.

  1. 1A build script

    A Blender 5.3 script writes each kitchen's plan: every fixture's place and size, its moving parts and grip points, and a physics description.

  2. 2Checked twice

    Two clean builds must agree: the same sizes, no equipment overlapping, the same file hashes.

  3. 3The Rust engine

    Heat, air, moisture and motion modules connect through shared ports and step together.

  4. 4Your browser

    TypeScript copies of four solvers run on this page, tested against the engine's reference kernels.

  • Built from code

    Every kitchen plan comes from a script, and the browser builds each room and every piece of equipment from that plan, so a change to the script rebuilds the kitchen exactly. Two clean builds of the plans must produce the same BLAKE3 hashes.

    0 overlapsSame hashes twiceBLAKE3

  • Pure Rust underneath

    Thermal, gas, solid and motion modules plug together through typed ports and run without C or Fortran libraries or a CAD kernel.

    fs-thermalfs-gasfs-solidfs-mbdfs-couple

  • Clear about its limits

    The fast delivery-box estimate refuses questions outside the range its table covers, such as a 42 °C loading dock, and hands them to the full model. It quotes its worst error, measured against the full model, not a guess. Fitted numbers, like k = 4.6 for crust crispness, are named as fitted.
